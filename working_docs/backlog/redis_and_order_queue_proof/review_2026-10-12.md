# Review of the First Implementation (2026-10-12)

An adversarial review of the archived branch `codex/redis-order-queue-feasibility` (39 commits, 271 files, +25.8k/−1.0k lines against `dev`) before restarting. This page keeps what the next implementation needs: the findings, what to port, and the traps. The decisions taken from it are in [design.md](design.md) section 3.1.

## Findings

- **The feasibility checkpoint was sound.** Six interleaved pairs per comparison, on a lab with three buy-only API processes and the stores on a separate Machine at about 0.8 ms:
  - Redis: PostgreSQL CPU down by a median 95.4 % (0.012 against 0.27 cores), all-request p95 15.9 ms against 51.6 ms, p50 about equal (4.2 against 4.5 ms). Preset: 1,000 arrivals/s for 10 s, stock 1.
  - Queue: winner p95 2.3 s against 33.7 s (×14.9), every buyer answered after 2.8 s against 35.1 s, 500 of 500 orders confirmed in both modes, ERP pace respected. Preset: 5,000-buyer spike, stock 500, ERP 100 ms and 20 TPS.
- **Both live comparisons of the implementation were artifacts:**
  - Without Redis, sold-out answers averaged 8 s. Every sold-out answer updated one counter row per run inside a transaction with a synchronous commit, so all rejections queued behind that row (about 2.5 ms each, 390/s against 1,000 arrivals/s). The lab, which wrote nothing for a sold-out answer and flushed counters in batches, measured a 52 ms p95.
  - Without the queue, about 390 of 500 orders ended failed without ever reaching the ERP: about 500 per-buyer loops polled PostgreSQL every ~100 ms on a pool of 10, starving dispatch to about 2 ERP calls/s against 19 allowed. The lab confirmed 500 of 500.
- **Robustness:** an ERP 503 or 429 during the inline window left the order in `processing` forever, because recovery refused to replay a call.
- **Scope:** the default production topology was changed (a new `checkout-surge-stores` app, three buyer APIs and a 16-CPU runner for every run), which would also have broken the CI deploy. The capacity calibration became a measurement apparatus (collector versions, ledgers, frozen analyzers) that produced no eligible result. A parent run with four children was built without being asked.
- **Protections were at parity** in both alternative modes: idempotency, run fence, bookkeeping, teardown. No overselling or idempotency bug was found.

## Worth porting from the archived branch

Port by reading the commits, then trim. Never merge the branch.

- Proof environment tooling and its production guard (`add0343d`), without anything from `fd49b672`.
- The control/buyer split concept (`381dcfaf`): one control process keeps every single-owner duty and buyers are stateless. Simplify its peer protocol: buyers look up a run's mode once; server timings may read "unknown".
- k6 latency per response outcome and the `200 order_confirmed` answer (`94f7ee1d`).
- Pieces of the comparison UI (`96b8994f`): disclosed cohorts, missing data shown as unknown, no invented winner. Rework its wording.
- The ERP client's deadline plumbing (`9246192d`), and the mock ERP's ledger reset fence if a reset race shows up.
- The PostgreSQL delivery queue's leasing (`24bb3dc5`, `966060ca`), with a short poll interval.

## Traps

- A sold-out answer must not write anything synchronously, in any mode. Counters are batched.
- Without Redis, stock, order and delivery row commit in one transaction. Do not copy the Redis mode's two-phase flow (holds, promotion, pending-persistence recovery): it guards a gap that does not exist inside one database.
- No per-request polling loops. Waiting requests share one notification or one batched check per interval.
- A buyer's deadline never fails an order. ERP calls are idempotent by order key, so recovery may replay them.
- Never let comparison work change ordinary runs, the production deploy, or the `CAPACITY_*` values.
- Compare equal cohorts. A mean over the buyers who got "yes" is not comparable with a mean over everyone.
- Measurement stays proportionate (design section 5). A run that completes cleanly is valid; do not build collectors, ledgers or frozen protocols.
- Do not run tests on the Windows host (design section 6).
